LeadVLALearning Follower-Aware Route Execution
for Robot Leading

Teaser video coming soon

LeadVLA enables a robot to actively lead a designated follower along a route, while adapting its pace and local motion to the follower’s evolving behavior.

01 / Method

Keep the route. Learn how to adapt.

The problem

Humans can lead someone along a planned route while naturally adjusting their pace and path as the follower slows down, stops, or falls behind. For robots, learning both abilities together in one model remains challenging.

Our idea

We separate the two jobs: keep the planned route as a stable guide, and learn when to slow down, wait, resume, or make small local adjustments.

01 — Path

Keep the route.

Use the planned route as a stable guide for where the robot should go.

02 — Pace

Control progress along the route.

Speed up, slow down, stop, or resume along the same guide route based on how the follower is moving.

03 — Residual

Adjust locally when needed.

On top of the paced route, make small local adjustments for the follower and nearby scene changes.

A closer look at key features of LeadVLA

Pause, wait, resume

The robot pauses when its designated follower stops for a phone call or a conversation. It keeps waiting even when the distractor walks away, and resumes leading when the target follower is ready to move again.

Lead and react to the right follower.

Same scene and route. A different instruction changes whose behavior the robot responds to.

Gray shirt, black shorts

The robot leads the person in the gray shirt and black shorts, adjusting its motion to this follower rather than the person in white.

Example instruction

Lead the person wearing a gray shirt and black shorts across the corridor, keeping them at a far distance and centered behind you.

White T-shirt, gray trousers

With the target description changed, the robot now leads and responds to the person in the white T-shirt and gray trousers, while the other person becomes the distractor.

Example instruction

Lead the person wearing a white T-shirt and gray trousers across the corridor, keeping them at a far distance and centered behind you.

Local route interference

The robot continues leading around an obstacle while responding to the designated follower in the white T-shirt and gray trousers—not the distractor.

The input route and instruction are unchanged from the white-T-shirt example above. The robot adapts its local motion to get around the obstacle without being given a new route.
